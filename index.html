Digital Strategy & EdTech Leadership

Kasia Suliga

Technology should enhance what makes us human - not replace it

I believe the future of education is not about choosing between humans and technology. It is about creating the right relationship between the two. Technology should remove barriers, extend possibilities and enhance learning - while human connection, communication, creativity, relationships and belonging remain at the heart of education.

I am particularly interested in digital strategy, AI in education, robotics, computational thinking, digital literacy, inclusion and human-centred technology.

My ambition is to help schools move from simply using technology to leading its purposeful, responsible and human-centred adoption.

Who I Am

About Me

I lead digital strategy and Computing across a primary school, developing the people, curriculum and technology needed to prepare children for a rapidly changing digital world.

Over the past four years, I have moved beyond delivering Computing lessons to designing and leading a whole-school approach to technology - from digital literacy and online safety in the early years to robotics, computational thinking, digital strategy and emerging AI. My work focuses on four interconnected areas:

01

Digital Literacy & Responsible Technology

I have established digital literacy as a whole-school priority from the age of three, ensuring children develop the knowledge, confidence and judgement to use technology safely and responsibly. I helped establish our school as a Common Sense Media School and lead initiatives that bring together pupils, parents and educators to explore online safety, parental controls, AI and responsible technology use.

02

Robotics & Computational Thinking

I designed and embedded a whole-school robotics programme from Reception to Year 6. Robotics is used not simply to teach coding, but to develop computational thinking, problem-solving, creativity, collaboration, communication and resilience. Children learn to work with technology while developing the human skills needed to work with each other.

03

Digital Strategy & School-Wide Change

I lead the development of our school's digital strategy, supporting the implementation of new technologies, developing policies and systems, delivering staff training and identifying opportunities for technology to improve teaching, learning and inclusion. My role sits at the intersection of curriculum, technology, and professional development.

04

AI, Research & the Future of Education

I actively explore the opportunities and challenges presented by AI and emerging technologies, with a focus on responsible adoption and meaningful educational impact. I work with external organisations, universities, foundations and local authority partners to share practice and ensure that pupils' experiences and voices contribute to wider conversations about the future of education and technology.

Publications & Partnerships

Working Together

A selection of the books, publications, media features and research partnerships I have contributed to or been featured in.

My expertise in digital education and technology has been featured in two publications, where I have been quoted on innovation and the evolving role of technology in education.

The Educator Magazine UK

New digital divide emerging between young people due to AI, warns education not-for-profit (OTS = 31,650) - This is a targeted B2B publication whose audience is predominantly Headteachers, SENCOs, and school business managers who receive the digital edition directly.

NOAH News

Micro:bit Foundation launches hands-on AI literacy initiative to prevent digital divide (OTS 5,000) - NOAH News is a UK digital news platform that monitors the news agenda and publishes stories on timely topics across a range of sectors.

"Many of our pupils interact with technology daily, but they are passively consuming it. It's vital that we equip them with this knowledge and skillset from a young age, as it's these foundational skills that will allow them to interrogate new tech such as AI and feel in control as technology advances, rather than feeling it controls them. By introducing physical computing early, children develop computational thinking through play. We don't begin with coding - we begin with curiosity. As a result, we find students no longer rely on the phrase 'it's broken' when a code fails, instead they analyse their errors, fix their code and test it again, a skillset that will be hugely beneficial to them throughout their life. Watching our pupils engage with the micro:bit demonstrates the untapped potential of children being able to create new and innovative ways of tackling real life problems. Our year 6 pupils worked with micro:bit and modular robots to create intelligent fans that reacted to rising temperatures, and seeing the joy and passion on their faces as they ran around the playground and showed how it worked in cool and hotter areas was absolutely incredible to see. Children are able to create magic, rooted in logic, if they're given exposure to the knowledge and tools like micro:bit and CreateAI."

Kasia's experience reflects the Foundation's wider belief that children should be equipped to understand and shape technology, rather than simply consume it.

Micro:bit Educational Foundation

The Micro:bit Educational Foundation is developing an Apple iPad and Android app for their educational product CreateAI. CreateAI is used with the BBC micro device to help children learn about computer programming and Machine Learning.

As part of their research with schools, I delivered a lesson using CreateAI and provided feedback to help the Micro:bit team ensure that the app and its educational content meet the needs of both teachers and students.

AI Digital Literacy in Partnership with University College London

I was delighted to work with Dr Emma Connolly, Fellow in Politics and Digital Civic Education at the Digital Speech Lab, University College London, on developing our pupils' understanding of AI and its impact on society.

Dr Connolly's team delivered a series of AI-focused sessions for our Year 5 and 6 pupils, exploring deepfakes, algorithms, AI-generated content and responsible AI use.

Partnerships like this are an important part of our digital strategy, bringing academic expertise into the classroom and giving pupils the opportunity to critically explore the technologies shaping their world.

Our aim is not simply to teach children how to use AI, but to help them understand how it works, how it can influence what they see and believe, and how to use it responsibly and critically.

ORChiD Project - Advisory Group

I am proud to have been invited by Dr Kasia Kostyrka-Allchorne, Lecturer in Psychology at Queen Mary University of London, to join the advisory group for the ORChiD (Online Risks and Resilience in Children's Daily Lives) project.

I will be working alongside Dr Kasia Kostyrka-Allchorne and her team, contributing my experience as a Computing and Digital Strategy Lead to this important research exploring how children aged 8-10 experience digital technology, the opportunities and risks it presents, and its impact on their wellbeing and development.

The project brings together children, parents, teachers and researchers to better understand children's digital lives and to develop practical tools and approaches that can support positive digital wellbeing, resilience and responsible technology use.

I am particularly excited to contribute the pupil, classroom and school perspective to this work and to help ensure that children's real-world experiences remain at the heart of research into the future of technology and education.

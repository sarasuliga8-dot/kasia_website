Who I Am

About Me

I lead digital strategy and Computing across a primary school, developing the people, curriculum and technology needed to prepare children for a rapidly changing digital world.

Over the past four years, I have moved beyond delivering Computing lessons to designing and leading a whole-school approach to technology - from digital literacy and online safety in the early years to robotics, computational thinking, digital strategy and emerging AI. My work focuses on four interconnected areas:

01

Digital Literacy & Responsible Technology

I have established digital literacy as a whole-school priority from the age of three, ensuring children develop the knowledge, confidence and judgement to use technology safely and responsibly. I helped establish our school as a Common Sense Media School and lead initiatives that bring together pupils, parents and educators to explore online safety, parental controls, AI and responsible technology use.

02

Robotics & Computational Thinking

I designed and embedded a whole-school robotics programme from Reception to Year 6. Robotics is used not simply to teach coding, but to develop computational thinking, problem-solving, creativity, collaboration, communication and resilience. Children learn to work with technology while developing the human skills needed to work with each other.

03

Digital Strategy & School-Wide Change

I lead the development of our school's digital strategy, supporting the implementation of new technologies, developing policies and systems, delivering staff training and identifying opportunities for technology to improve teaching, learning and inclusion. My role sits at the intersection of curriculum, technology, and professional development.

04

AI, Research & the Future of Education

I actively explore the opportunities and challenges presented by AI and emerging technologies, with a focus on responsible adoption and meaningful educational impact. I work with external organisations, universities, foundations and local authority partners to share practice and ensure that pupils' experiences and voices contribute to wider conversations about the future of education and technology.
